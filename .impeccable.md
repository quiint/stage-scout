## Design Context

### Users

Theatergoers and performers use Stagedoor to discover productions and preserve an exact account of the performance they saw or made: work, production, venue, date, cast, ticket, rating, and memory.

### Brand Personality

Contemporary playbill, discerning theater friend, and collected stage-door memento. It should feel theatrical and editorial without becoming costume-y or a generic social-product dashboard.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use the dark-house palette: oxblood marquee red, warm ivory ink, restrained brass highlights, and charcoal-paper surfaces. Pair Playfair/Libre Baskerville editorial moments with clear Inter controls. Posters carry the vivid color; controls stay tactile, continuous-cornered, and unobtrusive.

### Design Principles

1. Preserve canonical theater context and make the exact staging legible before asking for a reaction.
2. Prefer a printed program’s hierarchy—masthead, rules, venue/date/company metadata, and collected proof—over card grids or SaaS chrome.
3. Use tactile native controls, clear focus/selection states, and 44-point targets; do not use web-style substitutes.
4. Keep community contributions distinct from verified source-backed records.
5. Make the diary quick to complete, then let optional ticket, cast, and reflection details unfold progressively.
